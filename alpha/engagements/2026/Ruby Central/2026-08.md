## Package Repository Security

### Account Management

After last month's [security advisory](https://blog.rubygems.org/2026/07/22/security-advisory-legacy-api-key-leak.html) and a significant wave of spam account creation, the team set out to improve our account support and password recovery flows. Operators gained safer tools to delete accounts at users’ request, revoke API keys, perform targeted bulk account cleanup, and search blocked email addresses. Password-reset tokens are now consumed only after a password has been successfully changed—not when a reset link is opened. Links opened from email clients also work reliably while preserving RubyGems.org’s strict cookie protections. The team also backfilled missing WebAuthn identifiers for legacy accounts and removed model behavior that could interfere with password resets.

### Transparency Log

As this is the final month of Google Summer of Code, our contributor, Amisha, completed her work on the Transparency Log project. She added event-recorder integration, simplified payload construction around subject and resource attributes, streamlined the persisted event lifecycle, and advanced the historical backfill needed to connect existing gems with future events. As GSoC wraps up, we want to thank Amisha for all her work on the project. Colby will continue developing the Transparency Log, building on the foundation she created.

Elsewhere, the final major cleanup from the RubyGems.org redesign removed the old layout and styles. Trusted Publishing now verifies reusable GitHub Actions workflows more accurately, while invalid organization URLs return clean 404 responses instead of application errors. New contributor Issy also began addressing application errors and code-quality issues and started a broader effort to standardize log retention.

## AI Security Engineers in Residence

The SEiRs team continued to improve our Scrutineer automation harness.  This work is critical to our ability to scale the program.  The entry point to the scanning flow is automated and continues with minimal human input.  In August, we scanned 104 gems.  The team confirmed 11 vulnerabilities, with 4 reported and 7 pending (either due to internal review or awaiting maintainer contact info). Additionally, two libraries maintained by the Ruby core team have had findings handed over to be dealt with under their existing maintenance process.  We plan to disclose more detail once maintainers have had time to address these issues.

[Kou](https://github.com/kou), our Ruby Core member, turned his attention to scanning Ruby and its [39 bundled gems](https://github.com/ruby/ruby/blob/master/gems/bundled_gems#L9-L47) with Scrutineer.  He returned a total of 21 findings at the medium and low severities (these are not part of our numbers above).  So far, no critical vulnerabilities have been found, which is a relief given how prominent Ruby and its bundled gems are in the ecosystem. Next up is to work through all findings to validate and fix them. He's planning to fix non-critical findings to reduce future security reports from reaching the team.

A representative example is [ruby/rexml PR \#362](https://github.com/ruby/rexml/pull/362), which removes dead code that used `eval`. Code paths using `eval` are commonly flagged as vulnerabilities by AI scanning tools, even when the code is unreachable or otherwise not exploitable.

Dushan demonstrated our Scrutineer automation harness and scan backlog management pattern at a recent SEIRs standup and interest was expressed in it by the PHP team, we are considering open sourcing it.

Scrutineer contributions: Dushan implemented discrete [addressing of nested packages](https://github.com/alpha-omega-security/scrutineer/commit/f52e06da62359986746464235f5b590733ec0e7d) in monorepos (supports any language, not just Ruby). Patrick [improved disclosure drafting](https://github.com/alpha-omega-security/scrutineer/commit/c54297134316fefff21d67ded1fb8814dd48d901), merged many maintenance fixes and implemented some UI filtering improvements.
